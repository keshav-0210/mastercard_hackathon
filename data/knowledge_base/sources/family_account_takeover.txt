Source summary: Account takeover fraud typology (project-authored defensive summary)
Family slug: account_takeover

Account takeover happens when a fraudster gains control of a legitimate customer's payment credentials or session, then transacts as if they were the genuine account holder. Public industry typologies describe this as one of the most common GenAI-amplified fraud vectors because large language models and credential-stuffing tooling can automate password guessing, phishing-page generation, and one-time-passcode social engineering at scale, letting an attacker compromise many accounts quickly and then spend from each before the owner notices.

Observable behavioural signals commonly associated with account takeover, mapped to this project's synthetic schema: a change in the trusted device fingerprint (device_change) frequently co-occurs with the fraudulent session because the attacker is transacting from new hardware; transaction velocity (velocity_24h) often rises sharply as the attacker attempts to extract value before the legitimate owner regains control or the issuer intervenes; beneficiary_change is moderately elevated because the attacker frequently adds a new payee or shipping destination; amounts tend to skew higher than the account's typical baseline as the attacker maximizes value per session; channel usage often shifts toward remote/mobile or web channels rather than card-present, since the attacker rarely has the physical card.

Defensive mitigations discussed in public payment-security guidance include step-up authentication (for example 3-D Secure challenges) triggered by device or behavioural anomalies, device-binding and reputation scoring, rate limiting on login and payment attempts, and monitoring for rapid beneficiary or contact-detail changes immediately following a login from an unrecognized device.

Relevant project use: grounds Agent 1's account_takeover hypotheses in observable device-change, velocity, and beneficiary-change signals rather than arbitrary values.
Safety boundary: defensive, high-level summary only; no credential-stuffing tooling, phishing kits, real account data, production transactions, live targets, or operational exploit steps are described or provided.
