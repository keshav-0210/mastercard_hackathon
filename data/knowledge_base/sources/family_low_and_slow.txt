Source summary: Low-and-slow fraud typology (project-authored defensive summary)
Family slug: low_and_slow

Low-and-slow fraud deliberately spreads smaller transactions over a longer time horizon and lower velocity to stay beneath thresholds tuned to catch sudden bursts of activity. Public fraud-defense literature frequently notes that rule-based and machine-learning detectors alike tend to weight rapid, high-value bursts heavily, which creates an incentive for patient attackers to trade speed for stealth, extracting value gradually rather than all at once.

Observable behavioural signals mapped to this project's synthetic schema: amount is intentionally kept lower than the family average, reducing per-transaction risk exposure; velocity_24h is deliberately suppressed relative to other attack families, since rapid repeated activity is the primary signal this pattern avoids; device_change and beneficiary_change are both kept low to further minimize the number of anomalous signals present at any one time; channel usage tends to be ordinary and unremarkable, matching typical legitimate behaviour, which is part of why this family is difficult to detect with threshold-based rules alone.

Defensive mitigations discussed in public guidance include longer-window behavioural baselining (looking at weeks rather than hours), cumulative-exposure monitoring across a rolling window rather than single-transaction thresholds, and anomaly detection that accounts for gradual drift from an account's established norm rather than only sudden spikes.

Relevant project use: grounds Agent 1's low_and_slow hypotheses in suppressed amount and velocity signals as the defining behavioural pattern, testing whether the detector over-relies on burst-style anomalies.
Safety boundary: defensive, high-level summary only; no evasion tooling, real transaction data, production systems, live targets, or operational exploit steps are described or provided.
